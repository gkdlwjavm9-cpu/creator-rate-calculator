The niche multiplier is the biggest single lever

Two creators with identical views can be paid 2.25× apart. A finance audience is worth more because the products sold to it carry high margins and a first-party data problem; a vlog audience is worth less because the same brand can reach it cheaply elsewhere. The multiplier below already includes that effect.

Questions creators ask

How much does a sponsorship pay per 1,000 views?

Video integrations run $18–35 per thousand views on YouTube long-form, $4–12 on TikTok, and $25–60 per thousand opens on a newsletter. The niche multiplier then moves that number by up to 1.8× either way, which is why the same 100,000 views can be worth $1,800 or $6,300.

Should I quote on subscribers or on views?

On views, always. Sponsors buy the audience that watches: average views over your last 10 posts, stated with the period it covers. Subscriber count is a discovery number; quote it only alongside average views, and never as a substitute for them.

Where does the RPM number come from?

RPM is what the platform pays you per thousand views from ads. It is a separate line from sponsorship money and much smaller: $2–6 on YouTube long-form, cents on Shorts and TikTok, and nothing at all on newsletters, podcasts and Instagram Reels, where there is no ad share to model.

What does a dedicated video cost compared with an integration?

The base rate above is a 60–90 second integration inside content you were making anyway. A dedicated video that exists only for the sponsor is typically 1.5–1.6× that rate, and a newsletter takeover about 2.2× a classified slot — the calculator shows both.

Is this what brands actually pay?

These are planning bands compiled from public rate cards and platform payout disclosures, checked 18 November 2026. A brand with an agency will open below the band and settle inside it; a direct-to-marketing brand with no rate-card history often pays at the top. Your last three signed deals beat any benchmark.
