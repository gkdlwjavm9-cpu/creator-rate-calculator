YouTube Shorts sponsorship rates: what a brand pays per 1,000 views

A 3–5 second hook and one product moment inside a vertical short. Sponsors pay $3–8 per thousand views in a neutral niche, so 50,000 views is worth about $275 and 100,000 about $550. The platform's own ad RPM is $0.05–0.20 — a rounding error next to the sponsorship.

What we assume

One sponsor per post, a 60–90 second host-read or on-screen integration inside content you were making anyway, US audience, 30-day campaign window, no paid usage rights. Change any of those and the number moves — the rights clause alone is worth 20–50% and is priced separately on the rights guide.

How YouTube Shorts rates actually work

Shorts pay a fraction of long-form per view — the ad revenue share is measured in cents per thousand views, not dollars. Sponsorships are the real revenue line, and they are priced per thousand views at roughly a fifth to a quarter of the long-form CPM.

Because a single Short can spike to a million views and the next can do 4,000, brands buy them in bundles and ask for the median of the last 10–20 posts. Quoting the average of your last 20 Shorts, with a guaranteed minimum, is the version that survives contact with a media buyer.

The format constraint matters for the rate too: a 3-second mention is worth less than a 15-second skit built around the product, and most creators underprice the second one.

Best for

Reach at a low CPM. Volume, not rate, is the lever: sponsors usually buy a bundle of 3–5 shorts rather than one.

Watch out for

Views swing wildly, so quote an average and put a floor in the contract. Ad RPM is tiny on Shorts — the money is the sponsorship.

Sponsorship versus ad revenue, in one line

At 100,000 views the sponsorship is worth about $550 and the ad revenue on the same audience about $12.50 — you would need roughly 44 equally sized posts of ad revenue to match one sponsorship.

Questions

What is a YouTube Shorts sponsorship worth at 100,000 views?

Between $300 and $800 for a standard integration in a neutral niche, with $550 the realistic ask. Finance and B2B audiences multiply that by 1.7–1.8×; lifestyle and food bring it down to 0.8–0.85×.

How does YouTube Shorts compare with a newsletter per 1,000 people reached?

A newsletter gets $25–60 per thousand opens, several times this platform's band, because an inbox open is a real read and there is no competing algorithm. The trade is scale: a YouTube Shorts post reaches far more people per unit of effort.

Does YouTube Shorts pay ad revenue on top of sponsorships?

A small amount: $0.05–0.20 per thousand views. At 100,000 views that is about $12.50 per post, against a sponsorship in the thousands.

How many views do I need to earn a living on YouTube Shorts?

At the middle of the band and 12 posts a month, one sponsorship per post earns about $3,300 a month at 50,000 views. Two thirds of that cadence is a more realistic sell rate, so budget on the lower figure.
