Subscribers to views: the conversion nobody quotes honestly

The view rate band for TikTok is 30–150%, so a 1k-subscriber channel is expected to reach 300–1.5k views. The spread is wide on purpose: a channel that posts daily to a short-video feed reaches a large share of its followers, while a newsletter at the same subscriber count is doing well to get half of them to open.

Quote the figure from your own analytics, then use this band only as a sanity check. A sponsor who hears "we have 1k subscribers" and then sees 300 average views will renegotiate downward; one who hears the views first and the subscribers second treats the subscriber figure as upside.

Questions at this size

How many views does a 1k-subscriber TikTok channel get?

At the midpoint of the 30–150% view rate band, about 900 views per view (300 at the low end, 1.5k at the high end). Your own last 10 posts tell you where you sit — that figure is what to quote.

What should a 1k-subscriber TikTok channel charge per sponsorship?

$75 for an integration, $75–$75 across the band, before the niche multiplier. A finance or B2B audience pushes that to 1.7–1.8×; a lifestyle audience pulls it to 0.8×.

Is subscriber count or average views the better sales argument?

Average views, every time. Subscribers show reach potential; views show what a sponsor's money buys. Send both, but put the average views figure and the period it covers at the top of the email, and never let a subscriber count stand in for it.

What does this audience earn per month?

At the mid rate and one sponsorship per view at 12 posts a month, about $900 from sponsorships plus $0.65 in ad revenue — $901 in total if every slot sells. Plan on a two-thirds fill rate.
