Where these numbers come from, and what they leave out

Every figure on this site is a planning band, not a quote or an average of signed contracts. This page shows each band, how it is applied, and the four things that move a real deal away from it.

The formula

Sponsorship = average units ÷ 1,000 × CPM band × niche multiplier, rounded to the nearest $5 and floored at the platform's minimum deal. Dedicated = top of the band × the platform's dedication factor. Ad revenue = average views ÷ 1,000 × RPM band, at its midpoint. Effective CPM = the rate actually quoted ÷ units × 1,000.

Subscriber pages convert subscribers to units with the platform band at its midpoint, then run the same formula. That conversion is the weakest link in the chain: the real ratio depends on how your last ten posts performed, which is why every subscriber page says to quote your own analytics.

What is not in the model

Affiliate income. Often larger than the sponsorship for product-heavy channels, and earned per sale rather than per view.

Bonuses and programme income. Platform bonus programmes are invite-only and change without notice; they are not modelled, and the platforms that pay no ad RPM are shown as zero rather than a guess.

Production cost. A dedicated video that costs you two days is a different business from a 60-second read inside a video you were making anyway, even at the same headline rate.

Taxes, currency and agency commission. An agency takes 10–20%, international payments carry a conversion spread, and every fee here is pre-tax.

Four things that move a real deal

Exclusivity. Category exclusivity for 60–90 days costs 20–50% on top, because it removes your other bids. Paid usage rights. A brand running your video as a paid ad for 30 days should pay for it — typically 20–50% of the post fee. Whitelisting. Running the ad from your handle is a separate licence. Payment terms. Net-60 costs you the float; net-15 or a deposit is worth giving up a small discount for.

How to check a band against your own numbers

Open your analytics, take the median of your last ten posts rather than the mean, and multiply by the CPM band. If the answer is far above what brands have paid you, the gap is usually rights and exclusivity being bundled for free. If it is far below, your niche multiplier is doing the work and the page for your niche explains why. Neither case is a reason to discount the CPM — it is a reason to change what the deal includes.
